### Overview

A product case study on improving conversion for **small quick-commerce baskets**, where delivery fees and minimum-order thresholds can make otherwise useful purchases feel uneconomical. I identified the trade-off between immediate delivery and basket economics, then designed a lower-cost fulfillment path to recover orders without broadly subsidizing delivery.

### The Problem

Customers with small baskets can abandon their purchase when **delivery fees or minimum-order thresholds outweigh the value of the items**, creating a conversion gap despite strong purchase intent.

### The Insight

**The problem isn't always the basket size; it's the cost of fulfilling a small basket quickly.** Instead of forcing customers to add unnecessary items or subsidizing every order, the product can change the fulfillment trade-off for eligible small baskets.

### The Hypothesis

A **slower/consolidated delivery option** paired with **targeted, eligibility-based incentives**, giving customers a lower-cost path when instant delivery isn't essential while protecting unit economics.

### Impact & Validation

The MVP targets **higher small-cart conversion and incremental orders**, with **contribution margin, subsidy cost, and retention** as guardrails. The solution would be validated through an experiment comparing the new fulfillment path against the existing small-cart experience
